The person behind it all

Hanoi, 2004. My first business trip to Asia. At dinner, I had the honour of drinking cobra blood mixed with a local spirit. I threw my prejudices aside and gulped down the drink. I stifled a burp.

A year later, I sang karaoke in Bangkok with the local office staff. I cannot sing. I made a fool of myself. The others had fun at my expense. On Monday, I was greeted with smiles at the office. The ice between the Western manager and the local staff had been broken. I learned that for a team to succeed, I sometimes have to go against my own preferences.

I have worked in various middle and senior management roles across different industries, both at home and abroad. I also spent 16 years as an entrepreneur. In these roles, I have been an interpreter between engineers, programmers, managers and clients.

I am driven by a thirst for knowledge, a desire to understand how and why people and things behave as they do. I want to do work that benefits both the organisation and society. I care about the environment and want my work to reflect that. Solutions have to work in practice.

With people, I lead with feeling; with numbers, I lead with facts. The two are not mutually exclusive, but I need to keep thinking through the values behind my decisions.

When facing something new, I first ask myself whether I am looking for an answer to the right question. As a manager, I have not hesitated to make decisions and take responsibility for them. I am tolerant of others, but critical of my own work. Still, I accept that things do not always go right. We should not be afraid to experiment and try new things.

I am looking for work that combines innovation with the use of data. My plan is to write my master's thesis as part of the work, with the aim of becoming a permanent member of the team. I solve problems and put solutions into practice. I want to see what the work achieves and what difference the solutions make.

What needs to get done in your organisation? Let's talk about it! (I won't offer you a cobra drink, but I will offer you a cup of coffee.)
