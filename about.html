About me

Hanoi, 2004. My first business trip to Asia. At dinner, I was given the great honour of drinking cobra blood mixed with a strong local spirit. I threw my prejudices aside and gulped it down.

That same year in Hanoi, I joined the staff for a game of football. I am a terrible player and do not follow the game much. I had no trainers. It was 38 °C, and the humidity made my shirt stick to my back.

A year later, I sang karaoke in Bangkok. I cannot sing, and I made a fool of myself. Everyone had fun at my expense. On Monday, I was greeted with smiles at the office. The ice between the Western manager and the local staff had been broken.

I learned two things. Hanoi and Bangkok are not far apart, yet attitudes towards a Westerner differ. For a team to succeed, I sometimes have to go against my own preferences.

Preferences stem from feelings. With people, I lead with feeling; with numbers, I lead with facts. The two roles do not exclude each other. Working between them is my strength.

When working with numbers, I am guided by what can be defined, compared and checked against a source. Yet I find solutions intuitively, by making connections across different areas. My colleagues in Norway called me Potato, because potatoes work in almost any dish. A long-standing client nicknamed me MacGyver, because I find a solution even in a tight spot.

I have worked as a civil engineer and a software project manager. I also spent 16 years as an entrepreneur. In every role, I have been an interpreter between engineers, coders, managers and clients.

My guiding principle: when facing something new, I first ask myself whether I am looking for an answer to the right question. In decision-making, intelligence means efficiency; wisdom means doing the right things the right way. As a manager, I have not hesitated to make decisions and take responsibility for them. I judge other people's work leniently and my own strictly. Still, I accept that things do not always go right.

Could your organisation improve its use of data, its processes or its innovation activities? For me, it will be a master's thesis; for your organisation, a result. Let's do it together! (No cobra drink needed. Let's discuss it over a cup of coffee.)
